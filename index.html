Clemson IEEE Robotics

Every year, our team competes at the IEEE SoutheastCon competition.

IEEE SoutheastCon 2024

About the Competition

In this competition, teams design a fully autonomous robot that must navigate a complex obstacle course while completing multiple logistics tasks under a time limit. The robot’s primary objectives are to collect and sort packages into designated delivery zones, then retrieve fuel tanks and transport them across a hazardous gap (crater)—often requiring creative traversal methods like a zip line or bridge. After crossing, the robot must assemble the fuel tanks into a launch system and finally activate a button to stop the timer, completing the mission.

  • Embedded systems development
  • PCB design and layout
  • C/C++ programming
  • Hardware prototyping

CUHackit 2026

Every year, Clemson students organize and host the largest annual hackathon in South Carolina. It is a 24-hour event where students of all majors and experience levels collaborate in teams to rapidly design software or hardware projects.

Me and my team for this event enjoy playing the classic Catan board game in our free time. There is the standard physical board game with cardboard parts but there is also an online version that can be played from your phone or computer. We enjoy the faster pace of the online game, as well as not having to worry about counting out cards and distributing them to players each roll. Even with this, we still play the normal board game because it seems more personal and often leads to more involved players. Our idea was to build a standalone product that combines the best aspects of both worlds. A physical game that is automated to take care of the redundant processes for you.

My contributions

  • Soldered the various electronics used in the demo
  • Programmed an interface between the hardware and Pi/Pi Pico Microcontrollers in Python

Clemson Senior Design

This is a senior-level course in which students design, build, and test a comprehensive engineering project to solve a real-world problem.

Senior Design 1 (Fall 2025)

This year, the assigned project for students was to design a system that is capable of hitting all of the pins on a miniature bowling lane. The pins were 3D printed, the bowling ball was a metal bearing, and the lane was made of a rectangular sheet of plexiglass. In our design, the user could send a command to bowl using a GUI. A camera is mounted above the pins which detects which pins are set up using computer vision. The computer then automatically calculates the correct position to roll the marble to knock down the pins using a simple physics simulation. A solenoid is mounted to a PID controlled belt-driven linear motion system that drops the marble down a ramp when activated. After the pins are knocked down, a motor lifts the pins back into position automatically using strings.

My contributions

  • Designed and 3D printed the mechanism that reset the pins
  • Tuned the PID controller for precise movement
  • Wired the various electrical peripherals

Senior Design 2 (Spring 2026)

About the Competition

With the increasing use of unmanned aerial systems (UAS) in modern military operations, there is a growing need for modular payload deployment systems. The military has created the Picatinny Common Lethality Integration Kit (CLIK) standard to address this problem. The platform module is designed to interface seamlessly with up to four separate payloads. It provides power to each payload and communicates with the ground station via radio to relay important information including location, orientation, and the status of each payload.​

Key Deliverables

  • Interface with an external Aeronix Common Compute Module
  • Ethernet switching and routing for internal communication​
  • GPS positioning
  • Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) operation​
  • Internal power management system with electrical protections​
  • The ability to support up to four separate payloads​
  • 3D printed enclosure
  • Custom built 28V battery

  • Developed and implemented high-speed PCB layout constraints including impedance matching, differential pair routing, and length matching
  • Designed and defined the PCB stackup to ensure signal integrity and controlled impedance across high-speed interfaces
