Clemson IEEE Robotics

Every year, our team competes at the IEEE SoutheastCon hardware competition. This competition is hosted by IEEE, the Institute for Electrical and Electronics Engineers and is attended by students from dozens of university teams, with the aim of completing the most objectives for each year's competition.

IEEE SoutheastCon 2024

About the Competition

In this competition, teams design a fully autonomous robot that must navigate a complex obstacle course while completing multiple logistics tasks under a time limit. The robot’s primary objectives are to move small cubes into designated delivery zones, then retrieve small cylindrical "fuel tanks" and transport them across a ditch. After crossing, the robot must place fuel tanks into a stand and finally activate a button to stop the timer.

Our design useed a robotic arm to grab many of the cubes immediatly before pushing most of the smaller cubes with the front of the robot. Due to the complexity needed to carry the fuel tanks and reassembling them, we elected to finish with a quicker time by skipping this task. To cross over the ditch, four motors were added, two on the front and two on the back. These motors have small arms that, when rotated, pull the robot out of the ditch. This design is shown in the video above, and was able to place first in the competition out of over 40 university teams from across the Southeast.

My contributions

  • Programmed Pi and Pi Pico microcontrollers to interface with peripheral devices using I2C communication
  • Soldered a custom circuit board for organized signal and power distribution to robot components
  • Integrated IMU position estimates with device movement using C++
  • Hardware prototyping

About the Competition

In this competition, teams design a fully autonomous robot that are tasked with collecting scattered resources (“astral materials”) across a field and sorting them based on if they are magnetic ("Geodinium") or non-magnetic ("Nebulite"). The robot must gather materials from both open areas and an enclosed "cave". It also needs to transport containers to one of the pads to the left of the board, storing the resources in their respective containers. Extra points are given if the robot also places a custom made beacon/flag down and move the containers to a randomly selected pad.

Our design uses computer vision as well as an IMU (accelerometer and gyroscope) and motor encoders to accurately determine the position of the robot on the playing field. The resources are picked up and stored in the top of the robot using rollers that pull up materials in front of the robot. To optimize the number we collected, the robot was programmed to sweep across both the open area and cave area. Due to inconsistencies in sorting the materials, we elected to skip this step and optimize other aspects of our design. A small lever arm is used to grip containers that need moving, and the stored materials are moved to these containers through a lift mechanism that pushes the storage system over the output. This design is shown in the video above, and was able to place first in the competition out of over 40 university teams from across the Southeast.

My contributions

  • Programmed Pi and Pi Pico microcontrollers to interface with peripheral devices using I2C/UART communication
  • Used a Madgwick and Extended Kalman Filters to improve IMU performance and reduce the effect of noise
  • Integrated position estimates with device movement using MicroPython
  • Hardware prototyping

Clemson Senior Design

This is a senior-level course in which students design, build, and test a comprehensive engineering project to solve a real-world problem.

Senior Design 1 (Fall 2025)

This year, the assigned project for students was to design a system that is capable of hitting all of the pins on a miniature bowling lane. The pins were 3D printed, the bowling ball was a metal bearing, and the lane was made of a rectangular sheet of plexiglass. In our design, the user could send a command to bowl using a GUI. A camera is mounted above the pins which detects which pins are set up using computer vision. The computer then automatically calculates the correct position to roll the marble to knock down the pins using a simple physics simulation. A solenoid is mounted to a PID controlled belt-driven linear motion system that drops the marble down a ramp when activated. After the pins are knocked down, a motor lifts the pins back into position automatically using strings.

My contributions

  • Designed and 3D printed the mechanism that reset the pins
  • Tuned the PID controller for precise movement
  • Wired the various electrical peripherals

Senior Design 2 (Spring 2026)

This is an industry-sponsored senior design project focused on the development of a CLIK-compliant platform module for small unmanned aerial systems (UAS). In collaboration with industry sponsors, the project addresses the growing need for modular payload integration in modern military operations by implementing the Picatinny Common Lethality Integration Kit (CLIK) standard. The platform module is designed to interface with up to four independent payloads, providing regulated power delivery and real-time communication with a ground station via radio to report critical system information such as location, orientation, and payload status.

The system is built around a lightweight carrier-card PCB that interfaces with an Aeronix Common Compute Module and supports core sensing, communication, and power distribution functions. Key hardware features include LoRa-based communications, GPS and IMU integration, and a 28V battery-powered architecture designed to support multiple payloads simultaneously. The embedded software stack leverages FreeRTOS and existing Aeronix infrastructure to enable reliable peripheral control and system-level communication.

Key Deliverables

  • Interface with an external Aeronix Common Compute Module
  • Ethernet switching and routing for internal communication​
  • GPS positioning
  • Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) operation​
  • Internal power management system with electrical protections​
  • The ability to support up to four separate payloads​
  • Formal analysis report on an existing LoRa communication system performance
  • 3D printed enclosure
  • Custom built 28V battery

My Contributions

  • Performed schematic capture and PCB layout for the Ethernet subsystem, including switch, PHY, magnetics, and RJ45 connectors
  • Developed and implemented high-speed PCB layout constraints including impedance matching, differential pair routing, and length matching
  • Designed and defined the PCB stackup to ensure signal integrity and controlled impedance across high-speed interfaces
  • Applied best practices for high-speed design including minimizing crosstalk and avoiding split planes beneath critical signal traces
  • Integrated and adapted existing IMU and GPS reference designs from a previous team into the current system architecture
